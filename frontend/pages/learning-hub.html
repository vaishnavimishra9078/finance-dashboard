Stock Market Basics

A stock represents partial ownership in a company. When you buy a share, you own a small piece of that business and can benefit if it grows in value or pays dividends.

Stocks trade on exchanges like the NYSE or Nasdaq. Prices move based on supply and demand — driven by company performance, economic news, and investor sentiment.

Key terms:

  • Bull market — prices generally rising
  • Bear market — prices generally falling
  • Dividend — a portion of profit paid to shareholders
  • Market cap — total value of a company's shares (price × shares outstanding)

Key Metrics Explained

P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings): stock price divided by earnings per share. Shows how much investors pay per dollar of profit. Higher can mean growth expectations, or overvaluation.

EPS (Earnings Per Share): a company's profit divided by its number of shares. Higher generally signals stronger profitability.

Market Cap: total company value. Often grouped as small-cap, mid-cap, large-cap — affects volatility and growth potential.

Beta: measures a stock's volatility relative to the overall market. Beta of 1 moves with the market; above 1 is more volatile, below 1 is less volatile.

Diversification

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets, sectors, and geographies so no single loss can badly damage your whole portfolio.

The saying "don't put all your eggs in one basket" applies directly — if one stock or sector underperforms, others may offset the loss.

This is why the Risk Analyzer in this app scores your portfolio's concentration — a portfolio heavily weighted in one stock is riskier than one spread across many.

Risk & Return

Generally, higher potential returns come with higher risk. Savings accounts are low-risk/low-return; individual stocks are higher-risk/higher-return; leveraged or speculative assets are highest-risk.

Your personal risk tolerance depends on your timeline, goals, and comfort with volatility. Someone investing for retirement in 30 years can typically handle more short-term ups and downs than someone needing the money in 2 years.
